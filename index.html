How it works

🩻

1 · Scan in

A chest X-ray is converted to 28×28 grayscale, the MedMNIST format.

📐

2 · Features

Six measurable properties (brightness, contrast, edges, lung asymmetry) are extracted.

⚛

3 · Quantum circuit

Features become qubit rotations. Trainable layers with entangling CNOTs transform the state.

🎯

4 · Measure

Measuring qubit 0 gives the probability that the scan shows pneumonia.

Real data

Trained on 4,708 and tested on 624 real pediatric chest X-rays. Nothing is mocked.

Honest results

Dataset: PneumoniaMNIST

Real pediatric chest X-rays (frontal view) labelled Normal or Pneumonia, from the MedMNIST collection. It is derived from a public pediatric chest X-ray dataset (Kermany et al., 2018) and downsampled to 28×28 grayscale.

Class balance per split

Training data is about 74% pneumonia, so training uses class-weighted loss and the decision threshold is tuned on validation data only.

The quantum model

A variational quantum classifier: classical image features are encoded into qubit rotations, processed by trainable entangling layers, and read out by measurement.

Circuit (hover a gate to see its trained angle)

Features → qubits

Each feature is scaled to [0, π] using training-set percentiles and applied as an RY rotation.

What the circuit computes

After encoding and 3 layers of RY(θ) + a CNOT ring, we measure the Pauli-Z expectation of qubit 0:

P(pneumonia) = ( 1 − ⟨Z₀⟩ ) / 2

The circuit is simulated exactly on a classical computer (a 2ⁿ-amplitude state vector). It is not run on quantum hardware.

About the project

Problem

Reading chest X-rays for pneumonia needs expert radiologists, who are scarce in many regions. Automated screening could support earlier detection. This project explores whether a very small quantum circuit can learn that task.

Technology

PythonNumPyscikit-learnMatplotlibHTMLCSSJavaScriptGitHub Pages

Limitations

  • The quantum circuit is simulated classically, not run on quantum hardware.
  • Images are only 28×28, so fine detail is lost.
  • A classical model on the same features performs slightly better.
  • Trained on one pediatric dataset; it may not generalise to other populations or scanners.
  • Not a medical device. Do not use for diagnosis or treatment.
